A page can feel crowded before it contains very much. A label sits too close to a title. An image competes with its caption. Two ideas arrive without a pause between them.

Space gives these things a relationship. It can bring a caption close to the image it explains, while leaving a larger gap before the next thought. The reader gets a small clue about what belongs together.

## A pause with a purpose

For this journal, the aim is to make an article easy to choose and comfortable to stay with. A title needs enough room to be read. An excerpt needs to offer something beyond repeating the title. A photograph is useful when it adds context; a text-only entry should feel equally complete.

> Leave enough room for the thing you want someone to notice.

This is a working preference, not a rule about how every page should look. A dense reference table and a personal essay ask for different kinds of attention.

The practical question is smaller: if a gap disappeared, would the relationship become less clear? If a decorative element disappeared, would anything be missed? Those questions can make an edit more precise than simply asking for more white space.

Sometimes the next improvement is a sentence removed, a label moved closer, or an image allowed to stand on its own. A little space can be the result of deciding what matters.
